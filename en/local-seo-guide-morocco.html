Local SEO in 2026: get found before your competitors

When a customer searches "website design Marrakech" or "restaurant near me", they almost never scroll past the first three results. Local SEO decides, every single day, which businesses get the call — and which stay invisible. Here's what actually works in 2026 to dominate your local market, without an outsized advertising budget.

Y
Yassine Moughnagi Director of Strategy & SEO · Laval Digital
Laval Digital · SEO & Search

Local search accounts for 46% of Google queries

Nearly one in two Google searches carries local intent: finding a service, a shop or a professional nearby. For a business rooted in a particular place — Marrakech, Casablanca, or an entire region — it's the most qualified source of traffic there is. Someone typing "riad Marrakech medina" isn't browsing: they're ready to make contact.

The catch is that this intent plays out on a tiny piece of screen. Google's "local pack" — the map with three listings shown above the results — captures the overwhelming majority of clicks. Ranking fourth means sitting on a second page nobody scrolls to. Local SEO, then, comes down to winning those three spots, then defending them.

"We don't do local SEO to 'be on Google'. We do it to be the first name a customer sees at the exact moment they decide to buy."

— What we tell every client at the start of an SEO engagement

Google Business Profile: your most valuable SEO asset

Even before your website, your Google Business Profile (formerly Google My Business) listing is what determines your presence in the local pack and on Google Maps. It's also the most under-used lever: most businesses create their listing, fill it in halfway, then forget about it.

A listing that performs is a listing that's alive: an accurate primary and secondary category, exact opening hours, a defined service area, recent and genuine photos, a description rich in local keywords, and above all regular posts. Google favours active listings — one updated weekly sends a freshness signal that static competitors simply don't have.

Customer reviews: the fuel behind local rankings

1. Volume and consistency matter as much as the rating

Google reads your reviews as a signal of trust and relevance. A business with 120 reviews at 4.6 stars, collected steadily, will almost always outrank a competitor stuck at 4.9 with only 12 reviews from two years ago. Consistency wins out: three reviews a week beats thirty all at once followed by silence.

2. Reply to every review — good or bad

Replying to reviews isn't just good manners: it's SEO. Every reply is fresh content, often naturally rich in keywords ("Thank you for trusting us with your website redesign in Marrakech…"). And a measured reply to a negative review reassures future customers far more than a suspiciously perfect rating.

— How to earn reviews (without buying them)

  • Send a direct link to your listing by SMS or WhatsApp right after the service
  • Train your team to ask for one in person, at the right moment
  • Add a QR code to invoices, receipts or your shopfront
  • Never fake it: Google detects and penalises fake reviews

Optimising your website for local search

Your listing gets you into the local pack; your website wins the organic results just below it, and converts the visitor into a customer. Three areas of work drive most of the results.

Dedicated local pages. A page per city or per service-city combination ("marketing agency Marrakech", "e-commerce website build Marrakech") captures precise, high-intent queries — provided they're genuinely useful, not copies with the city name swapped out, which Google penalises.

Structured data. Schema.org LocalBusiness markup (address, phone, hours, service area, average rating) helps Google understand and display your business with rich results — stars, hours, directions shown directly in the search.

Consistent NAP. Name, Address, Phone must be rigorously identical everywhere: website, Google listing, directories, social media. The smallest inconsistency — an abbreviation, an old phone number — sows doubt with Google and dilutes your local authority.

Technical SEO: the invisible foundation

No content, however good, ranks on a broken technical base. In 2026, three criteria are non-negotiable — and often overlooked.

01
Loading speed (Core Web Vitals)

A site that takes more than 3 seconds to display on mobile loses half its visitors — and slides down the rankings. Compressed images, local hosting, caching: none of it is optional.

02
Mobile first

Google indexes your site's mobile version, not the desktop one. In Morocco, over 80% of local searches happen on a smartphone. If your site isn't flawless on mobile, nothing else matters.

03
Clean indexing

An up-to-date sitemap, no orphan pages, no duplicate content, HTTPS everywhere. Basics that 6 out of 10 sites we audit don't have properly in place.

04
Internal linking

Your pages need to link to each other logically. Good internal linking guides Google towards your strategic pages and spreads authority across the site — one of the most cost-effective levers available.

What to expect — and how soon

SEO isn't advertising: you don't buy a position, you build one. But unlike advertising, its effects last and compound. A pound invested in SEO still works two years later; a pound spent on ads stops existing the moment you stop paying.

On a well-run local SEO project, the first ranking movements appear within 6 to 8 weeks, with significant results on competitive queries within 4 to 6 months.

— Average observed across our SEO engagements in Morocco, 2024–2026

The good news for a local business: SEO competition is often light at city scale. Plenty of players settle for paying Google Ads without ever working on organic search. That's precisely where the opportunity sits — a position durably won on "your trade + your city" is worth years of free, qualified traffic. As the SEO agency in Marrakech businesses turn to for this exact work, we see it play out on nearly every audit.

Where to start

Our recommendation, after dozens of audits: don't try to fix everything at once. Start with what pays off fastest — your Google listing and your reviews — then move on to the website.

— Your next-30-days action plan

  • Claim and complete your Google Business Profile listing to 100%
  • Set up a simple, regular process for collecting reviews
  • Check your NAP is consistent across every online listing
  • Test your site's mobile speed (PageSpeed Insights) and fix the most urgent issue
  • Identify your 3 priority "trade + city" queries and build the dedicated page that's missing
